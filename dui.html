DUI & Impaired Driving

A momentary lapse in judgment can lead to severe, long-lasting consequences. A conviction for Impaired Driving, "Over 80," or Failure to Provide a Sample will result in a permanent criminal record, potential jail time, a mandatory minimum one-year driving prohibition, hefty fines, and potentially devastating increases to your insurance premiums. If you are facing these charges, an experienced DUI lawyer is essential to protecting your license and your livelihood.

Beating a DUI Charge in Ontario

Impaired driving law is one of the most technical and complex areas of the Criminal Code. A charge does not automatically mean a conviction. DUI cases are frequently won by exposing police errors and technical flaws in the Crown's evidence. A successful defence strategy often includes:

  • Charter Rights Violations: Filing applications to exclude evidence if the police unlawfully detained you, searched your vehicle without reasonable grounds, or failed to facilitate your right to speak to counsel without delay.
  • Breathalyzer Technicalities: Scrutinizing the calibration, maintenance logs, and operational procedures of the breath screening devices. If the machine was not properly maintained or operated, the readings can be excluded from trial.
  • Reasonable Doubt on Impairment: Challenging the police officer's observations regarding your sobriety, coordination, and driving patterns.
  • Negotiating Alternatives: When a trial is not the best option, aggressively negotiating with the Crown to reduce criminal charges to provincial traffic offences, such as Careless Driving, to save you from a criminal record.

DUI defences are highly technical and heavily rely on constitutional safeguards and procedural precision. Common trial strategies include:

  • Right to Counsel Violations (Section 10(b)): Proving the police intentionally or systematically delayed your access to a lawyer at the police station, rendering the breath samples inadmissible.
  • Unreasonable Search and Seizure (Section 8): Challenging the legality of the initial traffic stop or proving the police lacked the statutory grounds to demand breath samples.
  • Improper Maintenance / Calibration: Cross-examining the qualified technician to expose analytical gaps, improper temperature logs, or expired calibration checks on the Intoxilyzer instrument.
  • Lack of "Care or Control": If you were found in a parked vehicle, demonstrating that you had no active intention to set the vehicle in motion (e.g., sleeping it off safely in the backseat without keys in the ignition).

A first-offence conviction carries a mandatory minimum $1,000 fine, a 1-year criminal driving prohibition, an ignition interlock requirement, and a permanent criminal record. Additionally, you must complete Ontario's intensive "Back on Track" education and rehabilitation program at your own expense before your licence can be reinstated.
